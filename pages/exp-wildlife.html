About Wildlife Safaris

India is one of the world's most biodiverse nations, home to over 500 wildlife sanctuaries and 104 national parks. From the Bengal tiger stalking through Ranthambore's ancient ruins to the one-horned rhinoceros wading through Kaziranga's grasslands, a wildlife safari in India is a genuinely life-changing encounter with nature.

Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand is India's oldest national park and one of its most reliable for tiger sightings. Ranthambore in Rajasthan offers dramatic backdrops of medieval fort walls. Kanha and Bandhavgarh in Madhya Pradesh inspired Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book and remain prime big-cat territory. Each park has a distinct ecosystem and cast of wildlife characters.

Tips for Your Experience

Book safari permits 90–120 days in advance for popular parks like Ranthambore and Corbett
Early morning (6–9 AM) and late afternoon (3–6 PM) game drives have the best sightings
Wear muted earth tones — avoid bright colours that can alarm wildlife
A good wildlife guide makes an enormous difference to the quality of your experience
Monsoon season (July–September) closes most parks; October–April is peak safari season
