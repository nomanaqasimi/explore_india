About Goa Beaches

India's smallest state packs an extraordinary amount of personality into its 105 km of coastline. Goa's beaches range from the lively party strips of Baga and Calangute in the north to the serene white sands of Palolem and Agonda in the south. Centuries of Portuguese rule left behind a distinctive Indo-Portuguese culture: whitewashed churches, colourful colonial villas, and a cuisine that blends Konkani and Lusitanian flavours.

Beyond the beaches, Goa offers spice plantations, wildlife sanctuaries, and the haunting ruins of Old Goa — a UNESCO World Heritage Site featuring the Basilica of Bom Jesus, which holds the mortal remains of St. Francis Xavier. The state is equally famous for its seafood shacks, cashew feni (local spirit), and the electric energy of its beach parties and music festivals.

Travel Tips

South Goa beaches (Palolem, Agonda) are quieter and cleaner than North Goa
December–January is peak season; book accommodation 2–3 months in advance
Water sports — parasailing, jet skiing, kayaking — are all available on major beaches
The Saturday Night Market in Arpora is a lively mix of food, crafts and live music
Hiring a local taxi (not app-based) for a full-day tour costs around ₹2000–3000
