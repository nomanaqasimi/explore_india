🗺️  Trip Planning
The best time depends on where in India you're visiting. As a general rule, October to March is ideal for most of the country — the weather is cooler and drier, making sightseeing comfortable. North India's Himalayan regions are best visited from May to September for trekking, while the monsoon season (July–September) brings lush greenery to Kerala and the Western Ghats. Rajasthan's desert is most pleasant from November to February.
We recommend booking 3–6 months in advance for peak season (October–February) travel, especially if you want to visit during festivals like Diwali or Holi, or plan to take a Kerala houseboat. For wildlife safaris in popular parks like Ranthambore and Corbett, permit slots can fill up 90–120 days in advance. For off-season travel (April–June), 4–6 weeks is usually sufficient.
India is vast and endlessly varied — you could spend a lifetime here and not see everything. For a first-time visitor, we recommend at least 14 days to get a meaningful taste. The classic Golden Triangle (Delhi, Jaipur, Agra) takes 5–7 days. Adding Kerala or Goa requires another 4–5 days. For a broader journey across multiple regions, 3–4 weeks is ideal. Our itinerary planners can help you maximise any timeframe.
Most foreign nationals need a visa to enter India. The e-Tourist Visa (eTV) is available for citizens of over 160 countries and can be applied for online at indianvisaonline.gov.in. Processing typically takes 3–5 business days. Apply at least 4 days before your departure date. The e-visa is valid for 30 days, 1 year, or 5 years depending on the type you select.
✈️  Getting Around
India's transport network is extensive. Domestic flights are affordable and cover most major cities — IndiGo, Air India, and SpiceJet are the main carriers. Trains are an iconic and scenic way to travel, especially on longer routes; the Rajdhani and Shatabdi expresses are fast and comfortable. Book train tickets on IRCTC.co.in as early as possible. For shorter hops, private taxis arranged through your hotel or a trusted agency offer flexibility.
Yes — Uber and Ola operate in most major Indian cities and are generally safe, transparent on pricing, and trackable by GPS. They're ideal for city travel. In smaller towns or rural areas where apps have limited coverage, pre-negotiated auto-rickshaws or hotel-arranged taxis are the best option. Always share your ride details with someone you trust when travelling alone.
Self-driving in India is possible but not recommended for most visitors. Traffic is dense and chaotic by international standards, road rules are loosely observed, and signage is inconsistent. A far better option is to hire a car with a driver — it's affordable (typically ₹2,500–5,000/day), safe, and means you get a knowledgeable local companion. Scooter rental in Goa and a few other tourist-friendly areas is a popular and manageable exception.
💰  Budget & Costs
India suits every budget. A budget traveller can get by on ₹2,000–4,000 per day (approx. $25–50 USD) staying in guesthouses and eating local food. A mid-range traveller spending on comfortable hotels and guided experiences should budget ₹8,000–20,000 per day. Luxury travel — palace hotels, private tours, and fine dining — can run ₹30,000–1,00,000+ per day. Our planners can tailor an itinerary to exactly your budget.
Tipping is not mandatory in India but is warmly appreciated, especially in the tourism and hospitality sector. A tip of 10% at restaurants is standard if service charge isn't already included. For guides, ₹200–500 per day is a good benchmark. Hotel porters typically receive ₹50–100 per bag. Safari drivers and boat operators appreciate ₹200–500 per outing. Your generosity has a meaningful impact on people's livelihoods.
🏥  Health & Safety
Consult your doctor or a travel health clinic at least 6–8 weeks before departure. Commonly recommended vaccinations include Hepatitis A, Typhoid, and Tetanus. Hepatitis B is advisable for longer stays. Malaria prophylaxis may be recommended for rural or forest areas. Travellers arriving from yellow-fever endemic countries must carry a valid yellow fever certificate. Always travel with comprehensive travel insurance including medical coverage.

Always drink bottled water from sealed bottles (check the seal before purchasing) or use a quality water purification filter or sterilisation tablets. Be cautious with ice in drinks at smaller establishments, and avoid raw salads washed in tap water. Most mid-range and higher hotels provide complimentary bottled water in rooms.
Many women travel solo in India every year and have wonderful, enriching experiences. That said, it requires awareness and preparation. Stick to well-travelled tourist routes, book reputable accommodation, share your itinerary with trusted contacts, use app-based taxis, dress modestly in conservative areas, and trust your instincts. Cities like Rishikesh, Udaipur, and Kochi are generally considered very welcoming to solo female travellers. Joining a small group tour for at least the first trip can be a reassuring way to start.
