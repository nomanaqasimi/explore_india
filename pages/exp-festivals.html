About Festivals & Fairs

India celebrates more festivals than any country on earth — with over 36 public holidays and countless regional and local celebrations woven into the calendar, there is never a month without something remarkable to witness. Festivals here are not passive spectator events; they are immersive, full-sensory explosions of colour, music, devotion, and community.

Diwali, the Festival of Lights, transforms every city and village into a galaxy of oil lamps and fireworks. Holi, the Festival of Colours, sees strangers drench each other in bright powder in scenes of extraordinary collective joy. The Pushkar Camel Fair in Rajasthan (November) is part livestock market, part music festival, part spiritual gathering. The Kumbh Mela — held every 12 years at Prayagraj — is the largest peaceful gathering of human beings on the planet.

Tips for Your Experience

Book accommodation 3–6 months ahead for Diwali, Holi, and Kumbh Mela periods
For Holi, wear white clothes you don't mind ruining and protect your camera with a waterproof case
The Pushkar Camel Fair has a huge temporary tent city — book early for riverside spots
Local tourism boards publish annual festival calendars — use these to plan your trip
Participating respectfully in festivals rather than simply observing is always welcomed
