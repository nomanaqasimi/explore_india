About Food & Culture

Indian cuisine is not a single tradition but a magnificent tapestry of regional flavours, techniques, and ingredients shaped by thousands of years of history, trade, and migration. From the creamy Mughal-influenced kormas of Lucknow to the fiery chettinad curries of Tamil Nadu, each state — sometimes each district — has its own deeply distinctive culinary identity.

Food tours led by local guides take you beyond restaurant menus into the soul of a city: hole-in-the-wall chaat stalls in Old Delhi where recipes haven't changed in a century; the fish markets of Mumbai where the night's catch arrives at 5 AM; spice bazaars in Kochi where cardamom, pepper, and star anise perfume the air. Street food is where India's culinary creativity truly shines.

Tips for Your Experience

Follow the crowds for street food — the busiest stall is usually the best one
Ask your hotel or host to recommend neighbourhood dhabas over tourist restaurants
Vegetarian options are plentiful everywhere; India has the world's largest vegetarian population
Carry antacid tablets when trying very spicy food for the first time
Cooking classes in Jaipur, Kochi, and Varanasi offer hands-on learning with local chefs
